There's nothing like turning on the air con on a hot day and getting a face full of warm air. If your car's air conditioning has lost its cool, here's what's likely behind it, what each fix costs, and how to keep it working.

## Why it's blowing warm

The single most common reason is simple: **low refrigerant.** Car air con relies on a sealed gas that, over the years, slowly seeps out — so the air gets gradually less cold until one summer it's just warm. It's not necessarily a fault; it's normal ageing of the system.

But there are other culprits worth ruling out:

- **A refrigerant leak** — a faulty seal, hose or component letting the gas escape faster
- **A failed compressor** — the pump that circulates the gas; an expensive part
- **A blocked or damaged condenser** — often from road debris
- **An electrical or sensor fault** — stopping the system running properly

A diagnostic check tells you which of these you're dealing with before you spend money.

## What a regas costs

A **regas** removes the old refrigerant and refills the system to the right level, usually with a leak check. As a rough guide:

- **Older systems** (using R134a gas): around **£50–£90**
- **Newer cars** (using the pricier R1234yf gas, broadly 2017-onwards): noticeably more

Prices vary a fair bit by garage and gas type, so it's worth getting a couple of quotes rather than taking the first. If your air con has just gradually gone weak with no other symptoms, a regas often restores it completely.

## When a regas won't fix it

Here's the catch: if there's an actual **leak, a failed compressor or a blockage**, adding gas won't help — it'll either leak straight back out or fail to circulate. That's why a decent garage checks for faults *before* topping up, rather than charging you for gas that vanishes in a week. If you've had a regas and the cold didn't last, you likely have a leak that needs finding and fixing.

## Keeping it healthy

A few habits keep your air con working and head off bills:

- **Run it regularly — even in winter.** Using the air con for ten minutes now and then keeps the seals lubricated and stops them drying out and leaking.
- **Use it to demist** in cold, damp weather; it dries the air fast.
- **Regas every 2–3 years** as a matter of routine, as recommended by many manufacturers.

## Worth knowing: it's not an MOT item

Air conditioning **isn't part of the MOT**, so a weak system won't fail your test — but it also means nobody flags it for you. If it's not performing, treat it as a separate job and get it looked at, ideally before the summer heat arrives rather than during it. While you're booking a garage, you can also [check your car's MOT history and what's due](/mot-check) free with the registration, and line up any other work in one visit.

## The bottom line

Warm air con is usually just low refrigerant — a regas in the £50–£90 region often sorts it. But if a top-up doesn't last, you've got a leak or a failed part that needs proper diagnosis. Keep the system used year-round, regas every couple of years, and don't wait until the first heatwave to discover it's not working.
