If you have ever tried to book an MOT in late March and found nothing available for a fortnight, that was not bad luck. March is the busiest month for MOT testing in Britain by a wide margin, and the reason it happens is entirely mechanical — which means it is entirely predictable, and avoidable.

## The size of the spike

Around **2.5 million MOT tests** take place in March, roughly **50% above an average month**. September is the second peak at about **2.1 million**.

Those two months are not busy because of weather or holidays. They are busy because of the plate cycle.

## Why the plate change does this

New registration plates are issued twice a year, in March and September. Both are consistently among the biggest months for new car sales — March especially, frequently running at roughly double an ordinary month's registrations.

A car needs its first MOT three years after first registration. So every March cohort returns, together, three years later:

| Registered | First MOT due |
|---|---|
| March 2023 | March 2026 |
| **March 2024** | **March 2027** |
| March 2025 | March 2028 |

The same wave rolls forward every year, and it lands on the same finite number of testing stations. Layer on top the drivers who deliberately book in spring before summer trips, and garages in the second half of March can be booked out for weeks.

None of this is a surprise to the industry. It only surprises the driver who rings on the 20th.

## The one-month window, which almost nobody uses

This is the single most useful fact about MOT timing, and it is routinely misunderstood.

**You can test up to one month minus a day before your certificate expires without losing any time.** The new certificate runs from the old expiry date, not from the test date.

So a car expiring on **15 April** can be tested any time from **17 March** and still gets its full twelve months to 15 April the following year. Testing early costs you nothing. Not a day.

Which means the correct move, if your MOT falls in the rush, is to test at the very start of your window — before the last-minute wave arrives, while garages still have choice in their diaries and are still competing on price.

The people who end up waiting two weeks for a slot are, almost without exception, the ones who started looking after their certificate had already expired.

## How to actually avoid it

**1. Find out when yours is due.** Not approximately — exactly. A [free MOT check](/mot-check) shows the expiry date and the full test history against your registration, with no account needed. If the date falls anywhere between March and May, treat March as already busy.

**2. Book at the start of your window.** Count back one month from the expiry date and aim for that week.

**3. Compare rather than default.** The maximum chargeable fee for a standard car MOT is **£54.85**, but that is a cap, not a price. Plenty of garages charge well under it, and the spread between the cheapest and dearest garage within a mile of each other is routinely £20 or more. Our [cheap MOT comparison](/cheap-mot) shows what garages near you actually charge, which is far more useful in February than in the last week of March.

**4. Be flexible on the slot.** Weekday mornings and Saturdays go first. Midweek afternoons, early evenings and drop-off services tend to have availability when nothing else does.

**5. Do not fail.** A failure during the peak is the genuinely painful outcome — you need repairs *and* a retest slot, competing with everyone else for what is left.

## Thirty minutes that prevent most failures

Lighting, tyres, wipers and washers account for a large share of MOT failures, and nearly all of them are things you can check on your driveway.

**Lights.** The most common failure category by some distance. Walk round and test every bulb — headlights, indicators, brake lights, fog lights, reversing lights and the number plate light, which is the one everybody forgets. Bulbs cost a few pounds.

**Tyres.** The legal minimum is 1.6mm across the central three-quarters of the tread. Tyres sitting exactly at the limit are marginal and may fail on the day. Check for uneven wear, cuts and bulges too.

**Windscreen.** A chip or crack in the driver's line of sight is an automatic fail. A repair often costs £30 to £60 and is sometimes free through insurance — a windscreen replacement is neither.

**Last year's advisories.** Advisories are faults that were not bad enough to fail but were heading that way. Twelve months on, some of them will have arrived. They are listed in your [MOT history](/mot-check), and they are the single best predictor of what will fail this time.

## If the date has already passed

Do not drive it, except directly to a test you have already booked.

Driving without a valid certificate risks a fine of up to **£1,000**, and more importantly it gives your insurer grounds to decline a claim or to settle a third party's claim and recover the cost from you. The test costs at most £54.85; the exposure if something happens is open-ended.

Many garages hold back same-day or next-day slots for exactly this situation, so calling round is worth the twenty minutes. Our guide to [how much an MOT costs](/blog/how-much-does-mot-cost) covers what you should expect to pay when you have no time to shop around.
