A breakdown is one of those things you hope never happens — but if it does, the difference between a calm wait for recovery and a genuinely dangerous situation comes down to knowing what to do. Here's how to handle it safely, and the simple checks that stop most breakdowns before they start.

## The moment it happens

If your car loses power or develops a serious fault while driving:

1. **Get off the road if you can** — the hard shoulder, a layby, or a quiet side street. Keep as far from moving traffic as possible.
2. **Hazard lights on** straight away. At night or in poor visibility, leave your sidelights on too.
3. **Get everyone out and away from traffic** — through the doors furthest from the road, and stand well clear.
4. **Call for help** — your breakdown provider, or the emergency services if you're in a dangerous position.

The order matters: getting clear of moving traffic comes before anything else.

## On a motorway — the rules are different

Motorway breakdowns are the most dangerous, so the advice is specific:

- **Pull onto the hard shoulder** (or an emergency refuge area on a smart motorway), as far left as you can, with your **wheels turned to the left**.
- **Hazard lights on.**
- **Get out through the left-hand doors**, away from the traffic, and **get behind the safety barrier.** Never stay in the car and never stand in front of it.
- On a **smart motorway with no hard shoulder**, if you can't reach a refuge area, get as far left as possible, leave the vehicle if it's safe, get behind the barrier, and call for help.
- **Never** put out a warning triangle on a motorway — walking back along live lanes is far too dangerous.

On ordinary roads, a warning triangle placed at least **45 metres** behind the car helps — but only if you can set it up safely.

## What causes most breakdowns

The vast majority of breakdowns come from a short list — and nearly all of them give warning signs first:

- **Battery problems** — the single most common cause, especially after a car has sat unused.
- **Tyres** — punctures and blowouts, often from worn or under-inflated tyres.
- **Cooling and overheating** — particularly in summer traffic.
- **Electrical and engine faults** — frequently preceded by a warning light or odd noise.

## How to avoid one

Most breakdowns are preventable with basics most drivers skip:

- **Keep up with servicing** — it catches problems before they strand you.
- **Check your tyres** — tread, condition and pressures, including the spare.
- **Watch your battery** — have it tested if it's a few years old or the car sits idle a lot.
- **Don't ignore warning lights or new noises** — they're the car telling you something before it fails.
- **Keep fluids topped up** — coolant, oil, screenwash.

A car with a clean MOT, a full service history and no recurring advisories is far less likely to leave you on the hard shoulder. Before a long trip, it's worth [checking your car's MOT history and what's due](/mot-check) free with the registration — recurring advisories are exactly the niggles that turn into breakdowns.

## A note on breakdown cover

If you don't have breakdown cover, it's worth considering — a single recovery without it can cost more than a year's membership, and on a motorway it's about safety as much as money. Whether through your insurer, your bank account perks, or a standalone provider, knowing help is one call away takes the fear out of the worst-case.

## The bottom line

If you break down: get clear of traffic, hazards on, everyone out and behind the barrier, then call for help — and on a motorway, never linger in or by the car. Better still, prevent it: most breakdowns start with a flat battery, a tired tyre or an ignored warning light, all of which you can catch with a few minutes of basic checks.
