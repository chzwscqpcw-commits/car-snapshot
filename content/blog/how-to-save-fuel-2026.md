Pump prices may have stopped climbing, but they're still high — so getting more miles from every litre is money straight back in your pocket. The good news: most of the savings come from free changes to how you drive and maintain your car. Here are 12 that genuinely work, and a few popular ones that don't.

## How you drive (the biggest savings)

1. **Drive smoothly.** Gentle acceleration and braking save the most fuel. Hard launches and late braking burn it.
2. **Read the road ahead.** Anticipate traffic and lift off early instead of braking hard — you keep momentum and use less fuel.
3. **Keep a steady speed.** Constant small speed changes waste fuel; on the motorway, cruise control on flat roads helps.
4. **Ease off the motorway speed.** Wind resistance rises sharply above 60–65mph. Dropping from 80 to 70mph can noticeably improve economy on a long trip.
5. **Change up early.** In a manual, shifting to a higher gear sooner keeps the revs down and sips less fuel.
6. **Avoid unnecessary idling.** If you'll be stopped for more than a minute or two, switching off saves fuel (modern stop-start does this for you).

## How you maintain the car

7. **Check your tyre pressures.** Under-inflated tyres mean more rolling resistance and more fuel — a free, monthly five-minute win.
8. **Keep up with servicing.** A clogged air filter, old oil or worn spark plugs all hurt economy. A well-serviced engine runs more efficiently.
9. **Lose the dead weight.** Clear heavy clutter from the boot — the engine works harder to haul it around.
10. **Remove roof racks and boxes when not in use.** They add real aerodynamic drag; a roof box can dent economy on a long run.

## Plan and compare

11. **Combine trips.** A cold engine uses far more fuel for the first few miles, so several short separate journeys cost more than one longer combined one.
12. **Shop around for fuel.** Prices vary between stations and supermarkets — a fuel-price app can point you to the cheapest nearby and save a few pence a litre every fill.

## The myths that don't work

A quick reality check on popular "tips" that don't deliver:

- **Fuel-saving magnets, chips and most additives** — little to no proven benefit, and some risk harming your engine.
- **Running on near-empty to "save weight"** — the saving is negligible and you risk running dry and damaging the fuel pump.
- **Premium fuel in an ordinary car** — for most everyday cars, standard fuel is fine; premium rarely pays for itself.

## Know your real running costs

The biggest fuel saving of all is sometimes the car itself — a thirstier vehicle costs hundreds more a year than an efficient one. If you're weighing up a car, it's worth knowing its real-world costs before you commit. ## The bottom line

You don't need gadgets to use less fuel — you need a lighter right foot, properly inflated tyres, a serviced engine and a bit of planning. Stack those small habits together and the savings are real and ongoing, fill after fill.
