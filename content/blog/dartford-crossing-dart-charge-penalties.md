The Dartford Crossing is the busiest estuarial crossing in the country and one of the most reliable generators of penalty notices in Britain. Not because drivers refuse to pay, but because there is nothing at the crossing to pay at.

The barriers came down in 2014. What replaced them was a camera, a database, and a deadline most drivers do not know exists until a letter arrives.

## The deadline

**Midnight on the day after you cross.**

Cross on Tuesday afternoon and the charge is due by the end of Wednesday. Cross at 11pm on Tuesday and you still have until the end of Wednesday — the clock runs from the day of the crossing, not from the hour.

The standard car charge is modest. The penalty for missing it is not:

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Car crossing (pay as you go) | **£2.50** |
| Car crossing (pre-pay account) | Lower still |
| **Penalty if the deadline is missed** | **£70** |
| Penalty paid within 14 days | **£35** |
| Penalty unpaid after 28 days | **£105** |

The ratio is the point. A missed charge of a couple of pounds becomes a penalty roughly thirty times its size, and the only thing that changed was the date.

**Penalties are per crossing.** A day trip across and back, both unpaid, is two notices. Families who cross several times over a weekend without realising a charge applies can accumulate a genuinely alarming total from a single visit.

## Why so many people miss it

The crossing is free-flowing. There is no booth, no barrier, no gantry display telling you what you now owe, and no transaction at the moment it happens.

For a regular user that is fine — they know, and most run an account. The notices fall on people crossing for the first time:

- Holidaymakers routed over the bridge by a satnav that did not mention it
- Drivers who last used it when there were toll booths and assume there still are
- Visitors from outside the UK, who have no reason to expect a charge with no point of sale
- Anyone who crossed as a passenger-turned-driver and never registered the moment at all

There is also a timing trap. The charge applies between 6am and 10pm. Cross at 11pm and nothing is owed — which teaches some drivers that the crossing is free, right up until the day they cross at nine in the morning.

## How to pay, and how to stop having to remember

**Pay as you go** online at GOV.UK or by phone, by midnight the following day.

**A pre-pay account** is the practical answer for anyone who crosses more than rarely. It charges a reduced rate per crossing and settles automatically, which removes the deadline from your life entirely.

Accounts shift the failure mode rather than eliminating it. The two ways they go wrong are an expired payment card, and a vehicle that was never added to the account — a new car, a hire car, a courtesy car while yours is in the garage. In both cases the crossings are recorded against a plate the account does not cover, and they become unpaid exactly as if you had forgotten.

If you change your car, updating the Dart Charge account belongs on the same list as updating your insurance and your V5C.

## If a penalty notice arrives

You have **28 days** from the notice to make representations. Miss that and the right to challenge lapses and the full amount becomes due.

The grounds that succeed are overwhelmingly **documentary**:

- **It was paid.** A receipt, a bank entry, an account statement showing the transaction.
- **The vehicle was sold** before the crossing. Proof of sale and the date.
- **The vehicle was stolen** at the time. The crime reference number.
- **The notice was served late**, outside the permitted window.
- **The plate was misread.** Camera systems do misread — characters confused, plates obscured by a tow bar or a bike rack. If the notice describes a vehicle that is not yours, say so and ask for the image.

Grounds that reliably fail: not knowing the charge existed, not having been reminded, the satnav's choice of route, and being from outside the area. None of these are defences, however genuinely unfair they feel.

If Dart Charge rejects your representation, the next stage is the independent **Traffic Penalty Tribunal**, which is free to use and binds the authority.

## Ignoring it does not work here

Unlike a private parking charge, this is a statutory penalty with a statutory enforcement route behind it. Left alone it rises to £105, after which the debt can be registered with the court and enforcement agents instructed.

The distinction matters because advice about ignoring private parking invoices circulates widely and gets misapplied. A Dart Charge PCN is much closer to a council penalty than to a letter from a car park operator. Our guide to [private parking tickets](/blog/private-parking-tickets-what-you-owe) sets out where that line falls.

## When the notice is for a car you no longer own

If penalties arrive for crossings you did not make, the problem is usually not the crossing — it is the DVLA record.

Penalty notices go to the **registered keeper**. If a vehicle was sold and the V5C was never updated, the record still points at you, and so does every automated system that reads it: tolls, clean air zones, insurance enforcement and vehicle tax.

A [free car check](/car-check) shows what the DVLA currently holds against a registration. If a car you sold two years ago is still showing against your name, the Dart Charge notice is the least of what that record will eventually produce.

## The short version

- Pay by **midnight the day after** you cross — there is no barrier and no reminder
- Car crossings are **£2.50**, less on an account; the penalty is **£70**
- **£35** within 14 days, **£105** after 28
- Penalties are **per crossing**, so a return trip is two
- Charges apply **6am to 10pm** — crossing late at night is free, which misleads people
- An **account removes the deadline**, provided the card and the vehicle are current
- Appeal within **28 days**, with documents; then the Traffic Penalty Tribunal, free
