London's ULEZ gets the coverage, the politics and the headlines. Seven other English cities run charging zones of their own, and they work differently enough that assuming otherwise is an expensive habit.

The most common and costly assumption is that a Clean Air Zone charges cars. Most of them do not.

## The seven zones

| City | Class | Cars charged? |
|---|---|---|
| **Birmingham** | D | **Yes** — £8/day |
| **Bristol** | D | **Yes** — £9/day |
| **Bath** | C | No — commercial only |
| **Bradford** | C | No — commercial only |
| **Portsmouth** | B | No — commercial only |
| **Sheffield** | C | No — commercial only |
| **Tyneside** (Newcastle/Gateshead) | C | No — commercial only |

The class is the whole story. Zones run from A to D, and each letter adds a category of vehicle:

- **Class A** — buses, coaches, taxis and private hire
- **Class B** — the above, plus heavy goods vehicles
- **Class C** — the above, plus vans and minibuses
- **Class D** — the above, **plus private cars**

Only **Class D** charges ordinary cars. So a non-compliant diesel that would pay £8 crossing Birmingham passes through Bath, Sheffield and Newcastle for nothing at all.

That asymmetry is why "is my car compliant?" is only half the question. The other half is which city you are driving into.

## What makes a car compliant

The standard is the same across the English zones, and it matches London's ULEZ:

- **Diesel** — Euro 6 or better
- **Petrol** — Euro 4 or better

Translated into rough registration dates, that is diesels from around **September 2015** and petrols from around **January 2006**.

Treat those dates as a guide rather than the rule. The Euro standard is recorded against the individual vehicle, and manufacturers moved to new standards at different times — plenty of cars met the standard before it became mandatory. A 2014 diesel is not automatically liable, and the only way to know is to check the vehicle rather than the year.

## What it costs, and how it is charged

For a non-compliant **car** in a Class D zone: **£8 in Birmingham, £9 in Bristol**.

For heavier vehicles the numbers climb steeply — commonly **£50 to £100 a day** for buses, coaches and HGVs, which is the point of the scheme. These zones exist to move commercial fleets rather than to tax commuters, which is exactly why most of them leave cars alone.

Two mechanics worth knowing:

**The charge is daily, not per entry.** Driving in at nine, out at eleven and back in at four is one charge. There is no benefit to minimising trips once you have paid for the day.

**It applies to driving within the zone at all.** Passing through on a route to somewhere else incurs the same charge as a day spent inside it. There is no exemption for transit, which catches out drivers whose satnav takes a shortcut across a corner of Birmingham.

## If you do not pay

The zones are camera-enforced against the DVLA record. No barrier, no sign to stop at, nothing that tells you at the time that a charge has been incurred.

Miss it and a Penalty Charge Notice follows — typically **£120 plus the unpaid daily charge**, halved to **£60** if you pay within 14 days. The structure mirrors council penalties: pay quickly and it is manageable, ignore it and it escalates.

The practical difficulty is that nothing prompts you. Most zones give you a window after travelling to pay online, but you have to know you owe something. A driver who passes through Bristol once, unaware the zone exists, finds out several weeks later by post.

## The exemptions, and their limits

Most zones operate some combination of:

- **National exemptions** — disabled passenger vehicles, historic vehicles, certain specialist types
- **Local exemptions** — often for residents, local businesses or specific vehicle categories, usually time-limited and frequently now expired
- **Sunset periods** — temporary reprieves granted at launch, most of which have ended

The local schemes are where outdated advice does damage. Many were introduced for a year or two at launch and have since lapsed. If you are relying on an exemption you were granted in 2023, confirm it still exists before you rely on it again — the zones publish current exemption lists and they have shortened over time.

## Checking before you travel, not after

Three things decide whether you pay, and all three are knowable in advance:

1. **Your vehicle's Euro status** — not its age, the status recorded against it
2. **The class of the zone** you are entering — cars only pay in Class D
3. **Whether your route crosses the boundary** at all, including in transit

The first takes seconds with a [ULEZ and emissions check](/ulez-check). The second is in the table above. The third is the one people skip, and it is the one that produces the surprise letter six weeks later.

## The short version

- **Seven zones** outside London: Birmingham, Bath, Bristol, Bradford, Portsmouth, Sheffield, Tyneside
- Only **Class D** charges cars — in practice **Birmingham (£8)** and **Bristol (£9)**
- Compliance is **Euro 6 diesel / Euro 4 petrol**, judged per vehicle rather than by year
- The charge is **per day**, and **transit counts**
- Non-payment means a PCN of around **£120**, halved to £60 within 14 days
- Local exemptions have been quietly shrinking — check before relying on one
